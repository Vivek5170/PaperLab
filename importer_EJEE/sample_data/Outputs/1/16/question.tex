Consider a projectile subject to a drag force $\mathbf{F} = -m\alpha \mathbf{v}$. If it is fired with speed $v_0$ at an angle $\theta$, it can be shown that the height as a function of time is given by (just accept this here; it is one of the tasks of Exercise 3.53) $$y(t) = \frac{1}{\alpha}\left(v_0 \sin\theta + \frac{g}{\alpha}\right)\left(1 - e^{-\alpha t}\right) - \frac{gt}{\alpha}. \tag{1.19}$$ Show that this reduces to the usual projectile expression, $y(t) = (v_0 \sin\theta)t - gt^2/2$, in the limit of small $\alpha$. What exactly is meant by "small $\alpha$"?
